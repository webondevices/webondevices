"For the Big Mac can I have some fruit and white coffee. But I changed my mind, I want the chicken one large. Can I have water and fries with that? No pickles on the Big Mac, extra mayo on the chicken. Actually, make that a black coffee for the first menu."

Every drive-thru worker has heard an order like that. Most software would fall over. Ours untangled it and read the order back with a total, no-pickles, extra mayo and coffee swap included.

## Why we built it

The big fast-food chains have spent tens of millions on AI ordering. We wanted to know how close an independent team could get with GPT-4, the then-new function calling API and a few hours of focused work.

Answer: close enough to be interesting.

## How it works

**Personality from the staff handbook.** We pulled the rules, responsibilities and tone straight out of a real new-employee manual. Friendly, efficient, never pushy. (We skipped the section on uniforms.)

**Grounded in the real menu.** Every item, price, option and meal size went in, along with opening hours, location and breakfast times. So when someone asks for a breakfast muffin at 12:13, the assistant politely explains breakfast finished at 11.

**Orders as data.** Function calling defines exactly what an order looks like: items, sizes, sides, drinks, modifications. The model keeps the conversation human while filling in that structure behind the scenes. What comes out the other end is clean JSON a kitchen screen or POS could consume directly.

It handled upsells, totals, "actually, remove that," dietary tweaks and questions about what's in a burger. It was ready to test in less than a day, because it was built on our React AI assistant starter kit, which already had the chat interface and model connection in place.

## Beyond burgers

The same recipe fits anywhere people order, book or configure something by talking:

- **Restaurants and cafés**: drive-thru, kiosk, phone or table ordering
- **Retail**: guided product finders that turn a vague request into a basket
- **Hospitality**: room service, bookings and upgrades
- **Field services**: turning a customer's rambling description into a structured job ticket

The pattern is always the same. Brand voice and rules, grounded data, a strict output schema. The hard part is making it reliable at volume, with sensible fallbacks to a human, and that's the part we focus on. [Let's talk about your use case](/contact/).
