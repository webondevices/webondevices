Candidates hate applying into a void. Recruiters hate the void too. They're just buried under hundreds of applications per role, with no time to reply to everyone.

We built two small tools to fix both sides.

<Video youtube="-9Q_WTgM5NE" title="Pre-screening interviews and bulk CV processing with AI" />

## Tool one: the pre-screening chat

Before applying, candidates have a short conversation with an AI recruiter that has read the job description and their CV. It isn't trying to catch anyone out. It looks for deal-breakers: a missing core skill, a location that won't work, contract terms that don't fit.

We tested it on ourselves. Applying for a back-end Java role with a JavaScript CV, the interviewer asked fair, specific questions about Java, AWS, PostgreSQL and Kotlin, then declined kindly and with clear reasons. Applying for a front-end role, it picked up a subtle mismatch (we wanted remote, they said hybrid), asked about it, accepted "occasional visits to Gloucester, maybe once a month," and moved the application forward.

Both candidates walked away knowing exactly where they stood.

## Tool two: the bulk CV processor

Paste in a job description of any length, choose what to screen on (location, skills, experience), drop in the CVs and press go. Each takes a few seconds, so 500 applications take roughly three to five minutes.

Every candidate gets:

- A one-to-five rating
- A verdict: good match, weak match or not matching
- A short, specific explanation

In our test of 44 CVs, 21 matched and 23 didn't. The reasons read like a sharp recruiter's notes. One rejected candidate was a Python machine learning engineer applying for a JavaScript role. Another lived abroad with none of the listed skills. One strong match lost a star for not mentioning a skill the role asked for, and the summary said so.

## Doing this responsibly

Hiring decisions affect people's lives, so the rollout matters as much as the model.

- **Start with the obvious.** Location, right to work, contract type and availability are low-risk, objective filters, a sensible first step before anything involving judgement.
- **Explain everything.** Every decision comes with a reason a human can check and a candidate can understand.
- **Keep humans in the loop.** The tool sorts and summarises. People decide.
- **Test for bias** before and after go-live, on your own historical data.
