# Web on Devices — Website & Brand Project Brief

**Status:** Initial planning / ready for implementation  
**Company:** Web on Devices Ltd  
**Brand:** Web on Devices (WOD)

---

## 1. Project Overview

Web on Devices is evolving from a traditional software development and electronics website into a modern, multidisciplinary creative technology and engineering studio.

The new website should showcase a combination of:

- Hardware, electronics and connected physical systems
- AI applications, intelligent software and process automation
- 3D scanning, spatial media and immersive digital experiences
- Full-stack software engineering, web applications and interface design

The company combines established software engineering expertise with emerging technologies, experimentation and practical problem-solving.

**Core philosophy:** Build interesting, useful things by connecting software, intelligence and the physical world.

The website should not feel like a conventional software development agency, generic AI consultancy or hobbyist electronics blog.

It should feel like a sophisticated technology studio that designs, experiments, engineers and delivers working products.

### Primary objectives

1. Showcase completed projects, prototypes, experiments and original products.
2. Demonstrate technical credibility and engineering capabilities.
3. Attract potential clients for development, hardware, AI and automation work.
4. Establish a distinctive, contemporary brand identity.
5. Provide a flexible platform for regularly publishing new projects and ideas.

## 2. Brand Identity

### Brand name

**Web on Devices**

Use **WOD** as the primary visual shorthand.

The registered company name remains Web on Devices Ltd.

The website should not introduce additional businesses or separate brands for each discipline.

Instead, use a consistent naming system:

`WOD | Discipline Name`

### Logo

A new geometric WOD logo has already been selected.

**Important: The logo is final for this stage. Do not redesign or replace it.**

Use the supplied logo artwork as the source of truth.

The logo should:

- Work in solid black and white.
- Remain recognisable at small sizes.
- Work as a standalone symbol or alongside the company name.
- Support optional gradients without depending on them.
- Remain visually consistent across all four disciplines.

### Brand personality

The brand should feel:

- Modern and technologically progressive
- Intelligent and inventive
- Professional and technically credible
- Creative and experimental
- Minimal, confident and sophisticated
- Practical rather than excessively futuristic

Avoid generic AI-startup visual conventions, cartoon robots, brain illustrations, excessive circuit motifs or overly playful graphics.

### Brand positioning

Potential positioning statements:

- **Creative technology. Intelligent engineering. Real-world applications.**
- **Software, hardware and intelligence working together.**
- **Engineering ideas into reality.**
- **Technology beyond the screen.**

These are working options, not final approved copy.

## 3. The Four Core Disciplines

The website will be organised around four main areas.

These are disciplines within the same company, not independent brands.

### 3.1 WOD | Spatial Experiences

**Focus:** 3D capture, spatial media, immersive visualisation and interactive experiences.

Projects and services may include:

- Gaussian splatting and photorealistic 3D reconstruction
- Interior and exterior 3D property scanning
- Drone capture and aerial imaging
- Photogrammetry
- Property photography and visual presentation
- Digital property walkthroughs and virtual tours
- Interactive property websites
- 3D product presentations and configurators
- Virtual staging
- Digital twins
- WebGL / Three.js experiences
- Immersive web applications

**Positioning:** Capturing physical environments and objects, then transforming them into compelling interactive digital experiences.

The presentation of this discipline should be highly visual, using large photographic and 3D imagery.

### 3.2 WOD | Hardware & Electronics

**Focus:** Physical technology, connected devices, sensors, monitoring and intelligent electronics.

Clarity is particularly important here. Visitors should immediately understand that this area involves **real physical hardware and electronics**, rather than purely software systems.

Projects and services may include:

- Custom electronics and prototyping
- Arduino, ESP32 and Raspberry Pi applications
- Sensors and instrumentation
- IoT devices and connected equipment
- Remote monitoring
- Data acquisition and logging
- Environmental and agricultural monitoring
- Farm monitoring systems
- Hardware-integrated AI applications
- Edge AI and computer vision
- Smart home and building automation
- Robotics and physical computing
- Digital POS hardware and installations
- Interactive kiosks
- Digital screens and signage
- Out-of-home (OOH) advertising technology
- AI-enabled physical installations
- 3D-printed hardware enclosures and prototypes

**Positioning:** Bringing intelligence and connectivity into the physical world.

This division is an important differentiator for Web on Devices, especially as generic software and AI development become increasingly accessible.

Visual content should prominently feature real devices, electronics, prototypes, installation environments, sensors and physical interactions.

### 3.3 WOD | AI & Automation

**Focus:** Intelligent software products, AI applications, business automation and new approaches to digital workflows.

Projects and services may include:

- AI agents and assistants
- AI-powered search and recommendations
- BetterSearch and its vertical applications
- CarSearch — preference-driven vehicle discovery
- CareerSearch — interactive career exploration for teenagers
- AI voice receptionists and telephone agents
- Conversational interfaces
- AI-powered video chat and dynamic avatars
- Workflow and business process automation
- Document and data processing
- Integrations between existing systems
- Intelligent decision-support tools
- AI-powered customer service
- Automated business operations
- Experimental AI applications and original software products

**Positioning:** Using AI and automation to make complicated processes simpler, faster and more useful.

The focus should be on practical applications and working products, not generic AI hype.

### 3.4 WOD | Software Engineering

**Focus:** Professional software development, architecture, application engineering and interface systems.

Projects and services may include:

- Full-stack application development
- React and Next.js
- TypeScript and JavaScript
- Frontend architecture
- Backend services and APIs
- Cloud integrations
- UI/UX implementation
- Component libraries
- Design systems
- Accessible and responsive interfaces
- Interactive applications
- Web and mobile experiences
- Software architecture and technical consultancy
- Performance and maintainability
- Custom business applications
- Educational tools and simulations
- Modernisation of existing software platforms

**Positioning:** Designing and engineering robust, polished digital products from interface to infrastructure.

This discipline demonstrates the company's core professional expertise and provides credibility behind the more experimental work.

## 4. Projects, Products and Experiments

An important decision: **WOD | Labs will not be a separate fifth division.**

Experiments, prototypes, research projects and original products belong within the four existing disciplines.

Each discipline can contain a mixture of:

- Client projects
- Commercial products
- Internal products
- Experiments
- Research and development
- Technical demonstrations
- Prototypes and proof-of-concepts

Projects can span multiple disciplines.

For example:

| Project | Primary Discipline | Additional Tags |
|---|---|---|
| 3D property walkthrough | Spatial Experiences | Gaussian Splatting, WebGL |
| AI-enabled farm monitor | Hardware & Electronics | Sensors, AI, IoT |
| BetterSearch | AI & Automation | AI, Search, Product |
| AI voice receptionist | AI & Automation | Voice, Telephony, AWS |
| React component library | Software Engineering | UI, Design Systems |
| Digital POS installation | Hardware & Electronics | Touchscreen, Software |
| White blood cell RTS game | Software Engineering | Games, Simulation |
| Autonomous robotic prototype | Hardware & Electronics | Robotics, AI |
| 3D product viewer | Spatial Experiences | Commerce, Three.js |
| Business workflow integration | AI & Automation | APIs, Automation |

These are examples of potential portfolio content, not claims that all projects are completed.

### Project content model

Every project should have its own dedicated page, with structured fields such as:

- Title
- Slug
- Short description
- Hero image or video
- Primary discipline
- Additional disciplines and technology tags
- Project type (client, internal, prototype, experiment, product)
- Status (concept, in development, completed, live)
- Problem or opportunity
- Solution / approach
- Technical implementation
- Images, video, demos and interactive visualisations
- Results or outcomes, where available
- Relevant links

Avoid duplicating entire project pages when a project belongs to multiple disciplines.

The same project should be discoverable through different categories and filters.

## 5. Website Information Architecture

### Primary navigation

Suggested structure:

- Home
- Projects
- Expertise
- About
- Contact

The four disciplines can appear within Expertise, on the homepage and as filters in Projects.

Avoid overcrowding the top navigation with too many individual pages.

### Homepage structure

**1. Hero**

A confident, visually striking introduction to Web on Devices.

Include:

- The selected WOD logo and brand name
- Strong, concise headline
- Brief description of the company's focus
- Primary CTA to explore projects
- Secondary CTA to get in touch
- Distinctive supporting imagery or restrained interactive visual

The hero should explain the business without requiring visitors to understand the meaning of WOD.

**2. Selected Projects**

A visually engaging showcase of featured projects across different disciplines.

This is arguably the website's most important section.

Use large, high-quality visuals and short descriptions. Showcase what has actually been built rather than relying on generic service claims.

**3. Four Disciplines**

Introduce:

- Spatial Experiences
- Hardware & Electronics
- AI & Automation
- Software Engineering

Each should have its own visual identity within the shared brand system.

Use editorial-style photography or project imagery rather than four generic icons.

**4. About / Approach**

A concise explanation of the multidisciplinary approach.

Emphasise:

- End-to-end engineering
- Software and hardware expertise
- Experimentation and prototyping
- Modern technology
- Practical problem-solving
- Experience delivering production software

**5. Technology / Expertise**

A curated presentation of relevant capabilities and technologies.

Do not let a large wall of framework logos dominate the page.

**6. Contact / Collaboration**

An invitation to discuss projects, engineering challenges, product ideas and potential collaboration.

### Discipline landing pages

Each discipline should have:

1. Distinctive hero imagery and introduction
2. Core capabilities
3. Relevant featured projects
4. Technical approach
5. Related disciplines
6. Contact CTA

### Project detail pages

Project pages should feel like visual, editorial case studies rather than traditional blog posts.

Use a flexible layout capable of supporting:

- Full-width images
- Image galleries
- Videos
- Before/after comparisons
- Embedded demos
- 3D viewers
- Technical diagrams
- Code snippets
- Explanatory text
- Performance/results data

Not every project requires all of these elements.

## 6. Visual Design Direction

### Overall aesthetic

The intended visual style is:

**Sophisticated, minimal, highly visual and technologically progressive.**

The references shared during the branding process emphasised:

- Strong geometric branding
- Large, confident typography
- Sophisticated gradient treatments
- Carefully composed imagery
- Clean editorial layouts
- Generous negative space
- Strong alignment
- High-quality photography
- Modern product-design aesthetics

The website should feel intentionally designed, not assembled from a generic SaaS template.

### Design principles

**Negative space**

Use generous spacing around headlines, images and content blocks. Avoid crowded interfaces.

**Visual storytelling**

Let real project photography, renders, prototypes and working interfaces communicate capability.

**Controlled gradients**

Use gradients as a deliberate part of the identity, not as decoration on every element.

**High contrast**

Typography and interactive elements should remain clear and accessible.

**Restraint**

Avoid unnecessary borders, excessive cards, shadows, floating widgets and decorative icons.

**Consistency**

The brand should remain cohesive across all four disciplines.

### Colour palette

Blue is the primary brand colour.

Yellow and green provide complementary accents.

Suggested starting tokens (subject to design refinement):

| Role | Colour | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Primary blue | Vivid blue | `#2563EB` |
| Deep blue | Midnight blue | `#102A56` |
| Accent green | Fresh green | `#57C785` |
| Accent yellow | Warm yellow | `#F4C542` |
| Main text | Near black | `#111827` |
| Light background | Soft white | `#F8FAFC` |
| Dark background | Deep navy | `#0B1220` |
| Secondary text | Slate grey | `#64748B` |

These are proposed implementation defaults, not colours sampled from the final logo.

Use gradients combining blue with lighter blue, green and occasional yellow highlights.

Examples:

- Deep blue → vibrant blue
- Blue → cyan → green
- Blue with a subtle green or yellow glow

Avoid making the brand predominantly purple or relying on the familiar AI-startup purple/pink gradient treatment.

### Typography

**Primary typeface: Inter**

Use Inter consistently throughout the website.

Suggested principles:

- Large, confident headings
- Tight but controlled heading letter-spacing
- Comfortable body line-height
- Strong hierarchy
- Minimal unnecessary font weights
- Clear technical labels and metadata

A secondary monospace style may be useful for project metadata, technical specifications and code-related content, but should be used sparingly.

### Imagery

Use a deliberate combination of:

- Real hardware photographs
- Electronics and prototypes
- 3D spatial renders
- Architecture and property photography
- Software product screenshots
- Carefully composed conceptual imagery
- Subtle abstract gradients and graphic elements

Prioritise authentic project imagery wherever possible.

Avoid generic stock photography of people working on laptops, robot illustrations, random circuit backgrounds and imagery with no connection to the actual work.

### Layout

Design should be based on a consistent responsive grid.

Use:

- Large editorial image sections
- Alternating light and dark sections where appropriate
- Spacious two-column compositions
- Full-bleed featured visuals
- Asymmetric arrangements when intentional
- Strong typography
- Minimal UI chrome

The site's visual sophistication should come primarily from composition, typography, imagery and spacing rather than complicated effects.

### Motion and interaction

Potential enhancements:

- Subtle reveal animations
- Refined hover states
- Gentle gradient movement
- Image transitions
- Interactive project previews
- 3D demonstrations where relevant
- Scroll-based storytelling for selected case studies

Motion should enhance the experience rather than distract.

Respect reduced-motion preferences and prioritise performance.

## 7. Implementation Considerations

The exact technical architecture has not been finalised.

A sensible initial direction, given the company's existing expertise, would be:

- Next.js
- React
- TypeScript
- Inter
- Responsive CSS / design tokens
- Reusable UI components
- Structured, data-driven project content
- SEO-friendly project routes
- Optimised image and video delivery
- Optional Three.js / React Three Fiber for interactive 3D content

Consider MDX or a content management system for project case studies.

The initial version should be straightforward to maintain and expand.

Do not introduce unnecessary backend infrastructure simply to support a portfolio.

### Suggested route structure

```text
/
├── projects/
│   └── [slug]/
├── expertise/
│   ├── spatial-experiences/
│   ├── hardware-electronics/
│   ├── ai-automation/
│   └── software-engineering/
├── about/
└── contact/
```

This is a suggested information architecture, not a requirement for the exact routing implementation.

### Component considerations

Likely reusable components include:

- Site header and footer
- Hero sections
- Discipline cards
- Project cards
- Featured project layouts
- Image galleries
- Project metadata and tags
- Video and 3D media containers
- Section headings
- CTA sections
- Related-project navigation

The content structure should allow projects and disciplines to evolve without redesigning the site.

## 8. Content & Tone of Voice

The writing should be:

- Clear
- Confident
- Technically credible
- Concise
- Approachable
- Curious
- Free of unnecessary corporate jargon

Prefer explaining what a project does, how it works and why it's useful.

Avoid overused phrases such as:

- Revolutionary AI solutions
- Transforming the digital landscape
- Cutting-edge innovation at scale
- Empowering businesses through synergy

The website should demonstrate expertise through real work, not exaggerated claims.

It should feel personal and inventive without becoming an informal hobby blog.

- Refine gradient treatments.
- Improve image composition and page transitions.

### Phase 5 — Launch

- Review SEO and metadata.
- Optimise media assets.
- Test navigation and contact functionality.
- Check performance and accessibility.

Web on Devices should become a growing collection of interesting engineering work, products and experiments.

The site should support a shift beyond traditional contract software development into a broader creative technology business, combining professional engineering services with original products, connected hardware, spatial experiences and intelligent automation.
