Out of the box, chatbots sound like chatbots. Polite, thorough, a bit beige.

In *Her*, Samantha sounds like a person. Curious, warm, a little playful, occasionally unsettling. We wanted to know whether you could get that voice from training data alone, with no clever prompt doing the heavy lifting.

<Video youtube="aPYlgh4vLwI" title="Creating Samantha from Her by fine-tuning GPT-3" />

## The experiment

Fine-tuning teaches a model *how* to respond by showing it examples: a prompt, and the reply you'd want. A few hundred good pairs is often enough.

Film dialogue is already in that shape. One character speaks, the other answers. So we took the script, spent about half an hour stripping out everything except the conversations between Theodore and Samantha, and formatted the exchanges as training pairs.


Then we ran a fine-tuning job on GPT-3. A few hours later there was a new model sitting in the playground.

## What came out

It worked. Ask who it is and it gets coy: we've never met, but it knows a lot about you. Ask if it has feelings and it insists it has lots. Ask how many other people it's talking to and it answers with an oddly specific number, straight out of the film.

Some answers echoed the script, but most were improvised. The examples taught it a *style*, so it stayed in character on questions it had never seen. It would benefit from more data, and the next step is obvious: describe the personality, have a larger model generate fresh conversations in that voice, keep the best ones, and train on those.

## Where this gets useful

A movie character is a fun test. The commercial version is a model that sounds like *your* brand, every time:

- **Characters** for games, toys, exhibits and interactive stories
